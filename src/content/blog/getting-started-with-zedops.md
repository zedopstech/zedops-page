The fastest way to fail with new project software is to roll it out to every job at once. The fastest way to succeed is to pick one live project, move it across properly, and let the results do the convincing.

This is the plan we use with early customers. It assumes a live MEP or fit-out job that is already on site, and a team that is busy.

## Before you start: pick the right job

Not every project makes a good first project. Look for one that is:

- **Live but not in crisis.** Early enough that there is real work ahead, not so late that everyone is fighting fires.
- **Run by a project manager who wants it.** One motivated PM is worth more than any training plan.
- **Representative.** A typical job for your business, not a one-off. What works here needs to work on the next ten.

Avoid your largest project and your most troubled one. You want a clean test, not a rescue mission.

## Week 1: set up the project record

The goal of week one is simple. Everything the team needs to run the job exists in one place.

1. **Create the project** with its real structure: buildings, floors, zones or systems, whichever way your team actually talks about the work.
2. **Invite the core team** and give each person a role. Start with the PM, site engineers, the QS or cost lead and the storekeeper. Clients and subcontractors come later.
3. **Load the baseline.** Import the priced BOQ if you have one, and the current programme. They don't need to be perfect. They need to be the version the team is working to today.

> Don't migrate history. Last year's daily reports and closed RFIs can stay where they are. Start the new record from today.

## Week 2: move the daily habits

Software only sticks when it replaces something people already do. In week two, move the two habits that happen every day on site.

**Daily logs.** Site engineers file their daily report in ZedOps instead of on paper or WhatsApp: manpower, work done, photos, issues. It should take them less than five minutes. If it takes longer, simplify the template before you push harder.

**Material requests.** Requests from site go through ZedOps instead of phone calls to the store or procurement. Each request links to the BOQ item it belongs to, so the office can see what is being consumed against what was priced.

By the end of the week, the PM should be able to open one screen and see what happened on site yesterday without making a call.

## Week 3: connect the office

Now the site is feeding the record, bring in the people who use it.

- **Procurement** works from the material requests: raises POs and records deliveries against them.
- **The QS** tracks committed and actual cost against the budget that came from the BOQ.
- **Quality** runs inspections and logs snags in the same project, so issues have a location, a photo and an owner.

This is the week people usually notice the difference. The questions that used to need a meeting ("has that cable tray arrived?", "how much of the chilled water budget is committed?") now have an answer on screen.

## Week 4: review and decide

At the end of the month, sit down with the project team and ask three questions:

1. What do we no longer do by phone, email or spreadsheet?
2. What still lives outside ZedOps, and why?
3. What would we need to run the next project here from day one?

The answers tell you whether you are ready to add a second project, and what to set up differently when you do.

## What good looks like after a month

You are not aiming for perfection. A successful first month usually means:

- Daily logs are filed every working day, by the people on site.
- Material requests and POs are tracked in one place, against the BOQ.
- The PM runs the weekly progress meeting from the project dashboard.
- Nobody is keeping a parallel spreadsheet "just in case".
